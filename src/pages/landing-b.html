The problem

The guard that used to work

You know the passes coming at you. You have drilled the answers for years. But the frames arrive late now. The knee shield sits lower than it used to. The scramble you would have won at twenty-five goes the other way.

Your hips do not open the way they did. Something pinches in the front of the hip when you stand up after a round. Three days later you are still sore, and you are training around it instead of training.

It shows up in the small things first. Sitting cross-legged on the mat before class is uncomfortable, so you sit against the wall with your legs out. Getting your knee across on a body lock pass takes an extra beat. Inverting is something you used to do and now describe as something you used to do.

Then it shows up in the rounds. Your guard is a step behind, so you play it flatter. Flatter means heavier top pressure. Heavier top pressure means you get tired earlier, and tired is when the injuries happen.

The frustrating part is that nothing about your jiu-jitsu got worse. Your timing is better than it was five years ago. Your understanding of the position is better. The only thing that changed is the range your body will give you, and that is quietly deciding which techniques are still available to you.

The newer guy who taps you now is not better than you. He is just able to get into positions you have stopped being able to get into. You are solving the same puzzle he is, with a smaller range of pieces.

And you have adapted, which is the part that hides it. You picked the guards that do not ask much of your hips. You stopped inverting. You warm up longer. You train twice a week instead of four times, and you call that being sensible. Some of it is sensible. Some of it is managing a problem instead of fixing it.

None of that is a technique problem. You don't need another instructional. You need a body that keeps up with what you already know.

The reframe

This is not a yoga purchase

The goal isn't becoming better at yoga. The goal is becoming better at grappling. Nothing here is scored on how a position looks.

Which means the measure of whether this worked is not on a mat with a mirror. It is whether your knee shield holds where it used to, whether you can sit in the position long enough to work, whether the round after the hard round is still a round you want.

It also means the work is boring in the way that useful things are boring. There is no position to unlock and no photograph at the end of it. You do short sessions, repeatedly, and the range comes back slowly enough that you notice it in a roll before you notice it on the mat at home.

Not generic yoga. Not random stretching. Everything has a purpose, and the purpose is the eight collections below.

Who this is actually for

It is for you if your guard has got worse as your hips have got tighter, if the front of your hip pinches when you stand up, if you have stopped inverting, or if you cannot sit cross-legged without propping yourself up. It is for you if you intend to still be training in ten years and have started to wonder about that.

It is not for you if you are looking for technique. There is no guard instruction in here. It is also not for you if you want something to do instead of training... this sits alongside training, in the gaps, and it is built to take up very little room.

One more honest note. If you are dealing with an injury that a doctor is already involved in, keep them involved. Mobility work is not a substitute for that conversation, and nothing in these collections asks you to push through pain to get a result.
